# School Setup on Linux

This guide helps students use Linux for studying, note-taking, writing assignments, online classes, and research. It is written for beginners.

> **Documentation only:** This page explains the steps and links to official resources. It does not include installation scripts.

## Is this setup right for you?

Choose this setup if you want to:

- Take notes in class or while studying.
- Write essays and create presentations.
- Read and annotate PDFs and e-books.
- Join online classes and group calls.
- Research topics and manage citations.
- Learn programming or computer science.

## Before you start

- Check your school's software requirements. Some exam and proctoring tools may not support Linux.
- Ask your school IT help desk about supported apps before an exam.
- Back up important files. See [Linux system maintenance](../../guides/system-maintenance.md).
- Install Linux. See the [distribution list](../../README.md#distributions).
- Connect to the internet.
- Have your school login details ready.

## Setup steps

1. **Install Linux.** Choose a distribution from the [distribution list](../../README.md#distributions) and follow its installation guide.
2. **Update your system.** Install updates from your software center or package manager. See [Package managers](../../guides/package-managers.md).
3. **Install an office suite.** [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/download/download-libreoffice/) can open and save documents, spreadsheets, and presentations.
4. **Choose a note-taking app.** [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/download) is good for linked notes. [Joplin](https://joplinapp.org/download/) is good for simple notes and syncing.
5. **Set up PDF reading and annotation.** [Xournal++](https://xournalpp.github.io/installation/) lets you write on PDFs with a pen or mouse.
6. **Install a research tool.** [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/download/) helps you collect sources and create citations.
7. **Install a browser.** [Firefox](https://www.mozilla.org/firefox/) works with most school websites and online class tools.
8. **Test online class tools.** Open your school's video platform and test your camera, microphone, and screen sharing.
9. **Organize your files.** Make folders by semester and course. Keep assignments and notes in a place you can back up.
10. **Back up your work.** Use cloud storage or an external drive, and keep copies of important assignments. See [Linux system maintenance](../../guides/system-maintenance.md).

## Online class and exam note

Some schools use software that only runs on Windows or macOS. Do not try to bypass exam security or proctoring tools. Contact your school IT help desk and ask about supported options, a browser-based version, or a loaner device.

## Beginner tips

- Use clear folder names, such as `2026-Spring-Biology`.
- Back up notes and assignments often.
- Export important documents to PDF before submitting them.
- Test your camera and microphone before class.
- Use official apps and websites for school work.
- Keep your system updated for security and reliability.
